# Plan: a partner list instead of the typed Partner box

## Problem

When two partners have a club with the same name, the Club picker lists that
name once and the attendance form asks for the partner in a free-text box
(q331 "Partner"). Each of the two rosters is shown by a rule that needs the
picker to equal the club name **and** the box to equal the partner's full name
as the Roster spells it (`build_conditions`, `mk_term(PARTNER, "equals", …)`).
Jotform's `equals` ignores case and outer spaces, nothing else. A mentor who
types the acronym her organization uses ("HAFAI" for Health Aid For All
Initiative), the short form of a name with its acronym in brackets, or a
spelling with other inner spacing or accents sees no roster at all. That is
what happened to the Ruby mentor (Feedback 9).

Live state (read-only GETs, 2026-09-28):

| Form | Rules | Rosters | Shared names | Partner-gated rosters |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Africa 262382821971058 | 254 | 220 | 5 (Angels Club, Ruby, Shining Stars, Shinning Stars, Super Girls) | 10 |
| template 262352490535154 | 376 | 342 | the same 5 | 11 (plus Seeds of Hope Foundation's Shining Stars) |
| Asia, Latin America, U.S | 122 / 68 / 41 | 93 / 39 / 12 | none | 0 |

The partners concerned: Abaana Community Outreach (ACO) Africa, Binti
Network, Feminature Uganda Limited, Health Aid For All Initiative, Reach All,
Ready for Reading, World Economy Skills and Agro Development (WESAD), Youth
and Women Opportunities Uganda (YWOU) — and on the template also Seeds of Hope
Foundation.

## Options

**(a) More rules: one per partner alias.** A rule's terms are all-AND or
all-OR, so "(partner = A or B) and club = X" is one rule per alias. Derived
aliases (the bracketed acronym, the name without the brackets, the initials
with and without small words) plus a small hand-kept table. Africa would grow
by about 20 rules (254 → ~274), the template by about 22. Runtime behaviour is
already proven (several Show rules on one field). But it only fixes the
variants we guess: "Health Aid for all", "Binti", a typo or a different accent
still show nothing, and every new report means another alias. `form_status`
("exactly one show-rule per roster") and the sync's own reading of the
partner back from the rules (the last rule per question wins) would both have
to learn which rule is canonical. It would also deploy itself: the runner
applies the new rules with the next Africa roster change after the merge.

**(b) A partner list instead of the box (chosen).** One new dropdown per
form, generated by the sync like the Club picker: label "Partner", options =
the partners whose club name is shared with another partner on that form
(8 on Africa, 9 on the template), hidden until the picked club name is a
shared one, required when shown. The gated rules test the dropdown instead of
q331; q331 is hidden and no rule refers to it any more.

**(c) BACKLOG 36, partner → club two-step picker.** A partner dropdown (about
30 partners on Africa) and one club dropdown per partner (about 30 new
questions), with question 5 filled from whichever club dropdown is visible.
Every show rule changes, the Salesforce bridge's source for question 5
changes, and every mentor has to learn a new flow — for 10 rosters. It stays
the answer to "the 204-club list is too long", not to this problem.

**(d) One picker entry per partner for shared names** ("Ruby — Binti
Network"): no extra step at all, but question 5 receives the picker's text, so
keeping it "Ruby" would need a calculation with a literal club name or picker
`calcValues`, neither of which is verified on this runtime. Question 5 is what
Salesforce reads (CLAUDE.md rule 5). Not without a template experiment first.

### Why (b)

- **No typing:** it removes the whole class of failures (acronym, case, inner
  spacing, accents, spelling) instead of guessing variants.
- **Same rule count:** Africa stays at 254 rules and the template at 376; only
  the 15 / 16 partner rules (gated rosters plus "reveal the partner" rules)
  change the field they test.
- **Every piece is already proven on these forms:** a required plain dropdown
  with the "Please Select" placeholder, hidden until a Show rule fires, is
  exactly how the Module (q92, q93) and Session (q15, q95–q97) dropdowns work.
  `equals` on a dropdown's option text drives the year → module → session
  cascade. Several Show rules on one field is how q331 and q15 work today.
- **No translation write:** "Partner" and "Please Select" are already in
  `base`, `en` and all 11 other dictionaries on Africa (md5 keys checked
  read-only). Partner names stay English, like club names.
- **Question 5 is untouched:** the picker → question 5 copy does not change.
  The partner choice never reaches question 5, and the new question is not in
  the Salesforce mapping.
- **Nothing goes live on merge:** the code changes nothing on a form until
  someone runs `--partner-picker --apply` on it. The runner never passes that
  flag, and a form without the list keeps the typed box with the same rules
  (unit-tested).
- **Same pattern as the Club picker:** created once by a flag, recognised by
  its field name, its options and shape maintained on every run, audited by
  `form_status`.

## Design

- `glow/sync.py`: `PARTNER_PICKER_NAME = "partnerPicker"` and
  `partner_fields(questions)` = {q331} plus the list's qid when a form has it
  (the sync, `form_status` and `rename_club` all use it).
- `sync_attendance_form.py`:
  - `PARTNER_PICKER_PROPS` = label "Partner", no sublabel, `required` Yes,
    `hidden` Yes (only a rule shows it), `multipleSelections` No (a plain
    select: a mentor can pick only one), placeholder "Please Select".
  - `build_conditions(…, partner_field=PARTNER)`: the gated rules and the
    reveal rules test `partner_field`. The default keeps today's rules
    exactly.
  - `partner_choices(club_questions, partner_of, pending)`: the partners of
    every roster whose name (case-insensitively) is shared, plus the shared
    clubs about to be created. Deduplicated case-insensitively and sorted.
  - `--partner-picker` (once per form): creates the dropdown at q331's
    position, rewrites the rules, **then** hides q331. With that order a
    failure in between leaves the typed box working. Refuses when no club name
    on the form is shared (nothing to choose), and when a partner name
    contains `|` (Jotform's option separator).
  - When the form has the list, every run keeps its options and properties in
    shape (`UPDATE partner picker (N partners: …)`). The partner of each
    existing roster is read back from rules on either field.
  - When the form has shared names but no list, an info line says so (not a
    warning; the runner's bands ignore it).
  - The plan prints `conditions: K kept, N new, D dropped` whenever the rules
    are rewritten. That is the rule-level diff a reviewer needs for this
    change and for any later one.
  - `--summary-json` gains `partner_picker`.
- `tools/form_status.py`: a partner term may be on either field. When the
  form has the list, the audit also checks that every gated rule tests the
  list and not q331, that each tested partner is one of its options, that it
  lists no partner no rule tests, that it is required, and that q331 is
  hidden. The report shows `partner picker: q<id>`.
- `tools/rename_club.py`: the "shows the club only for a typed Partner"
  refusal also recognises the list.
- `tools/auto_sync.py`: an `UPDATE partner picker` line counts as a change, so
  an unattended run applies it like a Club picker update (and never creates
  one).

## Risks
- **The runner reads its code from `main`.** If the list were added from this
  branch while `main` still had the old sync, the next unattended Africa run
  would put every gated rule back on the now-hidden q331. Those 10 rosters
  could then never be shown. Mitigation: merge **before** any apply (as the
  Club picker wording did, af43d64), and set `AUTO_SYNC_APPLY=0` for the apply
  window.
- **A failure between the writes.** The list is created first and q331 is
  hidden last. A failed rules write therefore leaves the old rules and a
  visible q331 (still working); a failed hide leaves q331 visible, which
  `form_status` flags. The same command run again completes it (the list is
  found by name). A create response without a qid stops the run before the
  rules are written.
- **Hidden-but-required.** Jotform skips validation of a hidden field. This
  is the Module/Session dropdowns' live behaviour, so a mentor of a club with
  a unique name is never asked for a partner.
- **Default notification e-mails** are regenerated by Jotform when a question
  is added, as with the Club picker on 2026-09-12. Nothing reads q331 or the
  new question: not Salesforce (qid mapping), not the Apps Script.
- **Wrong partner picked:** no roster shows, as with a wrong typed partner
  today. It cannot happen silently for the mentor's own club, because every
  partner of a shared name is on the list by construction.
- **Rollback per form:** `restore_conditions.py --form <id> --from
  conditions-<stamp>.json --apply` (rules back on q331). Then un-hide q331
  (`question[hidden]=No`, or the builder) and delete the "Partner" dropdown in
  the builder. Then revert the merge, or the runner re-points the rules on the
  next run.

## Result (offline, 2026-09-28; nothing deployed)

Built on `wf/partner-box`. 80 Python tests, 20 of them new. They cover: the
default rules unchanged; the list changing only the partner field; the
`main()` flow on a fake form (create → rules → hide order, the qid-less
create, upkeep, read-back from rules on the list, both refusals before any
write); the `form_status` checks; `rename_club`; and the runner's parsing.
Three mutations were each caught by a failing test: dropping the pre-write
refusal, reading the partner back from q331 only, and hiding the box before
the rules write.

Read-only replay against the live forms (GETs only; club and partner names,
no roster). It rebuilds `club_questions` and `partner_of` from each form's
own questions and rules, using the club spelling the live rule tests (7
rosters on the template and 6 on Africa carry the Roster's lower-case spelling
in their rule, not the label's).
